<h2>1. Harvey AI — The Legal Reasoning Engine</h2>
<p>Harvey AI was built from the ground up for legal work, trained on legal data with input from elite law firms including Allen &amp; Overy. Unlike general-purpose chatbots, Harvey understands legal reasoning patterns, citation formats, and jurisdictional nuances. It handles contract analysis, regulatory research, litigation prep, and memo drafting across multiple practice areas.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Legal-specific training:</strong> Understands case law, statutes, and regulatory frameworks — not just general knowledge.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-jurisdictional:</strong> Works across US, UK, and EU legal systems with appropriate citation formats.</li>
  <li><strong>Security:</strong> Enterprise-grade data protection designed for attorney-client privileged material.</li>
  <li><strong>Workflow integration:</strong> Fits into existing legal workflows rather than requiring a new process.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Access:</strong> Not available to solo practitioners — primarily serves large firms and legal departments.</li>
  <li><strong>Pricing:</strong> Custom enterprise pricing makes it inaccessible for small practices.</li>
  <li><strong>Verification still required:</strong> Outputs need human review — it occasionally hallucinates citations.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> Custom enterprise pricing only. Contact Harvey AI for a demo.</p>

<h2>2. CoCounsel (Thomson Reuters) — Westlaw Meets AI</h2>
<p>CoCounsel is Thomson Reuters' AI assistant, deeply integrated with Westlaw's legal database. It performs legal research, reviews documents, drafts memos, and prepares deposition outlines. The key advantage is that every answer is grounded in Westlaw's verified legal database, reducing hallucination risk dramatically compared to general AI tools.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Westlaw integration:</strong> Answers are backed by the most comprehensive legal database available.</li>
  <li><strong>Citation accuracy:</strong> Links directly to verified cases and statutes — no fabricated citations.</li>
  <li><strong>Document review:</strong> Upload contracts and get clause-by-clause analysis with risk flagging.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Price:</strong> Expensive on top of existing Westlaw subscriptions.</li>
  <li><strong>Speed:</strong> Complex queries can take 30-60 seconds — slower than general chatbots.</li>
  <li><strong>US-centric:</strong> Best coverage for US law; international jurisdictions are weaker.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> From $100/user/month, bundled with Westlaw subscriptions. Volume discounts for firms.</p>

<h2>3. Casetext — AI Brief Analysis That Saves Hours</h2>
<p>Casetext, now part of Thomson Reuters, offers AI-powered legal research with a focus on brief analysis. Upload an opposing brief and Casetext identifies every cited case, checks whether the citations actually support the claims made, and suggests counter-authorities. For litigation attorneys, this feature alone saves 5-10 hours per brief.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Brief analysis:</strong> Automatically dissects opposing briefs and finds weaknesses in cited authorities.</li>
  <li><strong>CARA AI search:</strong> Describe your legal issue in plain English and get relevant cases ranked by applicability.</li>
  <li><strong>Parallel search:</strong> Finds analogous cases across jurisdictions automatically.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Overlap with CoCounsel:</strong> Since the Thomson Reuters acquisition, feature overlap creates confusion about which product to use.</li>
  <li><strong>Limited transactional use:</strong> Primarily built for litigation — less useful for corporate or transactional lawyers.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> From $110/month for individual practitioners. Firm pricing available on request.</p>

<h2>4. Spellbook — AI Contract Drafting in Microsoft Word</h2>
<p>Spellbook lives inside Microsoft Word as an add-in and helps lawyers draft, review, and negotiate contracts. Point it at a clause, and it suggests alternative language, flags unusual terms, identifies missing provisions, and generates new clauses based on your instructions. It was trained on billions of data points from legal contracts.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Works in Word:</strong> No new software to learn — it integrates directly into the tool lawyers already use.</li>
  <li><strong>Clause suggestions:</strong> Recommends standard market language when it detects unusual or one-sided terms.</li>
  <li><strong>Red flag detection:</strong> Automatically highlights provisions that deviate from market norms.</li>
  <li><strong>Speed:</strong> Reviewing a 30-page contract takes minutes instead of hours.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>English only:</strong> Does not support contracts in other languages.</li>
  <li><strong>General contracts:</strong> Works best for common contract types — highly specialized agreements may confuse it.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> From $99/month per user. Free trial available for qualified legal professionals.</p>

<h2>5. Luminance — Due Diligence at Machine Speed</h2>
<p>Luminance specializes in contract review for M&amp;A transactions, compliance audits, and portfolio management. Upload a data room full of contracts, and Luminance reads every document, extracts key terms, flags risks, and generates a summary report. What used to take a team of junior associates two weeks now takes Luminance a few hours.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Data room analysis:</strong> Processes thousands of documents in hours for M&amp;A due diligence.</li>
  <li><strong>Anomaly detection:</strong> Identifies unusual clauses by comparing against the rest of the document set.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-language:</strong> Reads contracts in 80+ languages — critical for cross-border deals.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Enterprise only:</strong> Built for large firms and corporate legal departments — not for solo practitioners.</li>
  <li><strong>Setup time:</strong> Initial configuration and training on your document standards takes effort.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> Custom enterprise pricing. Contact Luminance for a demo and quote.</p>

<h2>6. Lexis+ AI — Conversational Legal Research</h2>
<p>Lexis+ AI brings conversational AI to the LexisNexis legal research platform. Ask legal questions in plain English, and it returns answers grounded in LexisNexis content with linked citations. It also drafts legal documents, summarizes cases, and helps prepare for client meetings with quick legal overviews.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Grounded in LexisNexis:</strong> Every answer cites real sources from the LexisNexis database.</li>
  <li><strong>Conversational interface:</strong> Ask follow-up questions naturally — no need to construct Boolean searches.</li>
  <li><strong>Document drafting:</strong> Generates first drafts of memos, motions, and client letters.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Requires LexisNexis subscription:</strong> Not a standalone product — you need an existing Lexis account.</li>
  <li><strong>Occasional surface-level answers:</strong> Complex multi-issue queries sometimes get oversimplified responses.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> From $85/month as an add-on to existing LexisNexis subscriptions.</p>

<h2>7. Everlaw — AI-Powered eDiscovery</h2>
<p>Everlaw is an eDiscovery platform that uses AI to accelerate document review in litigation. Its predictive coding and AI-assisted review features help legal teams find relevant documents faster, reducing review costs by 50-70%. For firms handling large-scale litigation with thousands of documents, Everlaw is a game-changer.</p>
<h3>Why lawyers love it</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Predictive coding:</strong> AI learns from your review decisions and prioritizes the most relevant documents.</li>
  <li><strong>Story builder:</strong> Visualize case timelines and connections between documents, people, and events.</li>
  <li><strong>Cloud-native:</strong> No software to install — access from anywhere with a browser.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Limitations</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Litigation-specific:</strong> Built exclusively for discovery — not useful for transactional work.</li>
  <li><strong>Learning curve:</strong> Advanced features like predictive coding require training to use effectively.</li>
  <li><strong>Price:</strong> Per-GB pricing can add up quickly for document-heavy cases.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pricing:</strong> Custom pricing based on data volume and number of users. Free demo available.</p>

<h2>The Verdict</h2>
<p>For <strong>litigation attorneys</strong>, CoCounsel and Casetext are the strongest combination — Westlaw-backed research plus AI brief analysis. For <strong>transactional lawyers</strong>, Spellbook and Luminance cover contract drafting and due diligence. For <strong>large firms</strong> that want one platform across practice areas, Harvey AI is the most ambitious option. And for <strong>eDiscovery</strong>, Everlaw leads the field. The common thread: every tool still requires a human lawyer to verify outputs. AI handles the volume; you provide the judgment.</p>
