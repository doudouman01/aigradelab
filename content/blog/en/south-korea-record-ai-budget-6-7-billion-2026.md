<h2>Korea Nearly Triples Its AI Budget</h2>
<p>South Korea has allocated a record <strong>9.9 trillion won ($6.7 billion)</strong> for AI projects in 2026, nearly tripling its previous year's budget. The unprecedented spending commitment signals that Seoul views artificial intelligence as central to its economic future — not just a technology initiative, but a national strategy on par with semiconductors and shipbuilding.</p>
<p>The investment comes as the Boston Consulting Group's "AI Maturity Matrix 2026" placed South Korea in the top-tier <strong>"AI Leader" group for the first time</strong>, citing the country's domestic AI model development capabilities, computing infrastructure expansion, and public investment in AI ecosystem building.</p>

<h2>The AI Action Plan: 11 Major Initiatives</h2>
<p>In February 2026, the National AI Strategy Committee under the Presidential Office published the <strong>"Republic of Korea AI Action Plan" (2026-2028)</strong>, outlining 11 action items across four pillars:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Computing infrastructure:</strong> Building sovereign AI compute capacity, including next-generation data centers and GPU clusters</li>
  <li><strong>Data ecosystem:</strong> Establishing AI-ready data pipelines across government and industry</li>
  <li><strong>Security architecture:</strong> Creating a fundamentally new security paradigm for AI systems</li>
  <li><strong>Talent development:</strong> Training the next generation of AI researchers and practitioners</li>
</ul>
<p>The plan explicitly acknowledges that while Korean large enterprises and research institutions have rapidly adopted AI, the country's overall AI development investment lags behind what its economic position warrants — a gap the ₩9.9 trillion budget aims to close.</p>

<h2>Korea's 67-Regulation AI Reform</h2>
<p>On November 27, 2026, the Korean government announced a landmark <strong>"Regulation Rationalization Roadmap"</strong> at Naver's data center in Sejong. The roadmap identifies <strong>67 regulatory reforms</strong> across four categories: technology development, services, infrastructure, and safety.</p>
<p>The most significant reform addresses <strong>AI training data copyright</strong>. The government committed to publishing "fair use" guidelines by year-end and reviewing related legislation by 2026. This directly tackles one of the biggest obstacles facing Korean AI companies — uncertainty about whether using publicly available data for model training constitutes copyright infringement.</p>
<p>Other notable reforms include streamlined approval processes for AI medical devices, relaxed restrictions on autonomous driving testing, and simplified data sharing frameworks between government agencies and private companies.</p>

<h2>KAIST's Breakthrough: Visualizing AI Thought Processes</h2>
<p>In a significant research achievement, <strong>KAIST</strong> (Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology) became the first institution to visualize an AI model's reasoning process at the <strong>circuit level</strong>. Rather than treating AI as a black box, KAIST researchers mapped the internal pathways that activate during specific types of reasoning, creating a visual representation of how AI "thinks."</p>
<p>This research has profound implications for AI safety and interpretability — two areas where the global AI community has struggled to make progress. If researchers can see how a model arrives at its conclusions, they can identify potential biases, errors, or safety risks before deployment.</p>

<h2>The AI Startup Ecosystem</h2>
<p>Korea's AI startup scene is thriving, with several notable developments in November alone:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pikadi</strong> raised ₩1.6 billion for its AI short-form video creation platform that can analyze long-form content and create short-form clips in 3 minutes</li>
  <li><strong>Vibers</strong> secured ₩1.1 billion in seed funding for its enterprise AI transformation (AX) solution "Maestro," serving finance and healthcare clients within 3 months of founding</li>
  <li><strong>SemiAI</strong> received seed investment from Kakao Ventures for its semiconductor yield optimization technology that creates virtual fab data and uses AI to identify defect causes</li>
  <li><strong>Squeezbits</strong> gained recognition at NeurIPS for its AI model compression technology</li>
</ul>
<p>Meanwhile, Kakao announced <strong>Kanana Space</strong>, an AI agent integrated into KakaoTalk that can send messages, manage schedules, and perform searches — with selected user access beginning December 2026.</p>

<h2>The Digital DNA Initiative: Protecting Celebrities from Deepfakes</h2>
<p>In a uniquely Korean development, the <strong>Korea Entertainment Management Association</strong> partnered with M83 to extract and store celebrities' facial and voice data as <strong>"Digital DNA."</strong> Only pre-registered official data would be authorized for content creation, aiming to prevent unauthorized deepfake synthesis and identity theft — a growing concern in Korea's entertainment industry.</p>

<h2>Korea's Global AI Positioning</h2>
<p>At the APEC summit in November, President Lee Jae-myung proposed an <strong>"AI Basic Society" initiative</strong>, calling for technology innovation that drives inclusive growth across the Asia-Pacific region. Korea also hosted the second Korea-Japan AI Working Group, discussing safety standards, intellectual property, and cross-border AI governance.</p>
<p>According to the Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index, Korea produced 5 "notable" AI models in 2025, ranking <strong>third globally</strong> behind the US (50) and China (30) — ahead of Canada, France, and the UK (each with 1).</p>
