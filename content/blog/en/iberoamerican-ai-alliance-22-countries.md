<h2>Iberoamerica Wants Its Own AI</h2>

<p>At the <strong>XXX Iberoamerican Summit</strong> held in Madrid on November 4-5, 2026, representatives from 22 countries agreed to create the <strong>Alianza Iberoamericana de Inteligencia Artificial</strong> — a multilateral alliance dedicated to building AI technology adapted to the needs of the Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking world.</p>

<p>The core problem the alliance addresses is stark: according to the Iberoamerican Secretary-General Andrés Allamand, <strong>AI is 90% driven by English</strong>. That means the AI models powering chatbots, search engines, translation tools, and business software think, reason, and generate in English first — and treat Spanish, Portuguese, and indigenous languages as secondary afterthoughts.</p>

<h2>What the Alliance Will Do</h2>

<p>The Iberoamerican AI Alliance focuses on three areas of cooperation:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Secure data sharing:</strong> Creating frameworks for member countries to share training data that reflects their languages, cultures, and institutional knowledge</li>
<li><strong>AI model development:</strong> Building and fine-tuning language models that natively understand Spanish, Portuguese, and regional indigenous languages — not just translated English models</li>
<li><strong>Shared computing infrastructure:</strong> Pooling computational resources so that smaller countries can participate in AI development without building massive data centers independently</li>
</ul>

<p>The Dominican Republic proposed hosting an Iberoamerican AI center, signaling that the alliance is moving beyond declarations toward concrete infrastructure.</p>

<h2>Why Language Matters in AI</h2>

<p>The language gap in AI is not just a cultural issue — it has practical consequences:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Lower quality outputs:</strong> AI models trained primarily on English data produce lower-quality results in Spanish and Portuguese — more hallucinations, worse grammar, less cultural accuracy</li>
<li><strong>Economic dependency:</strong> If Spanish-speaking businesses rely entirely on English-first AI tools, they become consumers of foreign technology rather than creators of their own</li>
<li><strong>Underserved populations:</strong> Roughly <strong>700 million people</strong> speak Spanish or Portuguese as their primary language. Indigenous languages spoken across Latin America are almost entirely absent from AI training data</li>
<li><strong>Bias amplification:</strong> Models trained on English data carry English-language biases about culture, law, healthcare, and business practices that may not apply in Latin American or Iberian contexts</li>
</ul>

<h2>The Competitive Landscape</h2>

<p>The Iberoamerican alliance joins a growing number of regional AI initiatives:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>EU AI Gigafactories:</strong> Seven AI computing centers funded by €11.4 billion, with France, Italy, and Spain among the candidates</li>
<li><strong>India's IndiaAI Mission:</strong> Launched domestic AI foundation models EKA-1, Dhīr 2B, and Dhīr 5B</li>
<li><strong>Japan:</strong> SoftBank and OpenAI joint venture SB OAI Japan for localized enterprise AI</li>
<li><strong>China's WAICO:</strong> A new international AI governance organization with global participation, including Brazil</li>
<li><strong>Arab world:</strong> UAE and Saudi Arabia investing heavily in sovereign AI infrastructure</li>
</ul>

<p>The pattern is clear: countries and regions are recognizing that relying entirely on US- and China-built AI creates strategic vulnerabilities.</p>

<h2>What This Means for Users</h2>

<p>For the 700 million Spanish and Portuguese speakers worldwide, the alliance could eventually deliver:</p>

<ul>
<li>AI assistants that understand regional dialects, slang, and cultural context</li>
<li>Legal and medical AI tools trained on local regulations and practices</li>
<li>Educational AI platforms designed for Latin American and Iberian curricula</li>
<li>Business tools that work natively in Spanish and Portuguese without quality loss</li>
</ul>

<h2>The Bottom Line</h2>

<p>The Iberoamerican AI Alliance is a bet that the future of AI should not be monolingual. With 22 countries, 700 million speakers, and growing compute investments, the Spanish and Portuguese-speaking world is building its own seat at the AI table.</p>
